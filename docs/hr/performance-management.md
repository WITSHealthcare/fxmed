# FXMed Performance Management

This document sets out how FXMed agrees goals with staff, reviews their work and handles under-performance: the probation review at the end of the 90-day new-hire plan, the yearly goal-setting, mid-year and annual appraisal cycle, a single rating scale, and role-specific KPIs for clinical and sales staff. FXMed earns patient trust by recommending only what a patient needs, so no KPI here rewards ordering more tests, diagnosing more conditions, selling more supplements or upgrading more patients to bigger programmes. It applies to all employees and, in a lighter form, to locums and contractors.

Owner: Compensation and Performance Manager (to assign)

## The year at a glance

| When (Lagos time) | What | Who | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 90 of employment | Probation review | Supervisor + Admin | Confirm, extend or end probation (written) |
| January (by 31 Jan) | Goal setting | Each staff member with their manager | 3–5 goals + role KPIs agreed and recorded |
| Monthly | 1:1 check-in (30 min) | Manager | Short note: progress, blockers, wellbeing |
| Quarterly | Case review (clinical) or call/chat review (sales) | Peer or supervisor | Audit score and feedback (see training plan "Ongoing") |
| July (by 31 Jul) | Mid-year review | Manager | Progress on goals, no formal rating, adjust goals |
| December – mid January | Annual appraisal | Self-review, manager, calibration | Final rating, development plan |
| February | Calibration and pay review | Admin + managers | Ratings moderated; raises and promotions (see `compensation-and-payroll.md`) |
| 1 March | Pay changes take effect | Payroll | New salaries on March payroll |

Staff who join after 30 September take part in the next cycle's goal setting but are not rated in the current annual appraisal; their probation review stands in for it.

## 1. Probation

Probation runs alongside the 90-day plan in `docs/new-hire-training-plan.md`. The day-90 sign-off there is the probation review.

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Length | 3 months for all roles (to confirm: 6 months for Lead/Head roles). Stated in the offer letter |
| Notice during probation | As stated in the contract (suggested: 1 week either way) (confirm with counsel) |
| Check-ins | End of week 1, day 30, day 60, day 90 (as in the training plan) |
| Extension | Once only, by up to 3 months, with a written list of what must improve and by when |
| Outcome | Confirmed / extended / not confirmed, written and signed, given to the employee within 5 working days of day 90 |

### Probation review checklist

| Area | Evidence | Pass looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Training | Training tab progress % and quiz scores | Foundations + role track 100%; privacy quiz 100% |
| Competence | Supervisor sign-off survey; supervised cases or leads reviewed | Clinical: at least 10 supervised cases signed off. Sales: at least 15 leads reviewed |
| Professional standards | Licence verified (clinical); no breaches of privacy or "what we may say" rules | Licence current; zero confidentiality breaches |
| Reliability | Attendance, punctuality on home visits, response times | No unexplained absences; home visits on time or patient told in advance |
| Fit with how we work | Feedback from buddy, colleagues, patients | Courteous, honest, asks for help |

A serious breach during probation (for example sharing patient data on a personal WhatsApp) is handled through the disciplinary process, not the probation review.

## 2. Goal setting

Each person has:

- **Role KPIs** (section 4), the same for everyone in that role, and
- **3–5 personal goals**, written so that someone else could tell whether they were met (what, by when, how we will know).

At least one goal is a development goal (a skill, a course, CPD hours). Clinical staff include their licence renewal and CPD as a goal every year.

Good: "Complete the Hormone Balance protocol lesson and run 5 supervised Hormone Balance follow-ups by 30 June, with no documentation gaps in the case review."
Not allowed: "Increase lab test orders by 20%" or "Sell the Elite programme to 10 patients."

Goals are recorded against the staff member in the HR system and visible to them and their manager.

## 3. Rating scale

One scale for KPIs, goals and the overall rating.

| Rating | Label | Means |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Outstanding | Well above what the role asks, consistently, and lifts the people around them |
| 4 | Strong | Above expectations on most goals and KPIs |
| 3 | Meets expectations | Does the job well. This is the expected rating for most staff |
| 2 | Partly meets | Some goals or KPIs missed; needs a development plan |
| 1 | Does not meet | Clearly below the role; needs a Performance Improvement Plan |

Rules:

- **Overall rating** = manager's judgement, informed by roughly 60% KPIs and goals and 40% behaviours (patient care, honesty, teamwork, data protection). It is not an arithmetic average.
- **Ethics override**: a substantiated breach of clinical ethics, patient privacy or the "what we may say" rules caps the overall rating at 2 for that year, whatever the numbers say.
- **Calibration**: managers meet in February and compare ratings across departments before they are final. No forced distribution; with a small team it would be meaningless.
- The employee sees and can comment on their rating before it is final, and can ask for a second review by the Admin (see "Disagreeing with a rating").

## 4. Role KPIs

KPIs are measured mostly from records already in the portal (CRM, follow-ups, appointments, Training tab, FM analysis) plus quarterly reviews. Targets below are starting points (to confirm with the clinical lead and sales lead after the first quarter of data).

### Clinical team (doctors, nurses, pharmacists, lab scientists, dietitians)

| KPI | Measure | Starting target | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Documentation | Consult notes completed within 24 hours of the visit or call | ≥ 95% | Healthcare / clinical CRM timestamps |
| Follow-up completion | Scheduled follow-ups done on or before due date (Lagos date) | ≥ 90% | Follow-ups |
| Clinical audit | Quarterly case review score (history, rationale for tests, interpretation against `docs/fm-reference-ranges.md`, plan, safety-netting) | ≥ 80%; no "unsafe" items | Case review |
| Appropriate investigations | Every test ordered has a documented clinical reason | 100% in audited cases | Case review |
| Appropriate referral | Red flags escalated or referred on the same day | 100% | Case review, incident log |
| Patient experience | Post-visit survey score (1–5) | ≥ 4.3 average | Patient survey |
| Home-visit punctuality | Arrived within the booked window, or patient told before it started | ≥ 90% | Appointments |
| Licence and CPD | Practising licence current all year; CPD hours as the council requires | 100% / on track | HR licence record |
| Training | Annual privacy refresher and any "what changed" lessons done | 100% | Training tab |

**Never used as targets for clinical staff:** number of tests ordered, lab revenue, supplements dispensed or recommended, programme enrolments or upgrades, number of diagnoses or "dysfunctions" identified. These figures are watched only as **guardrails**: in the quarterly review the clinical lead looks for outliers in either direction (a clinician ordering far more or far fewer tests per patient than peers) and reviews a sample of those cases. An outlier is a prompt to look, not a mark against anyone.

### Sales and patient growth team

| KPI | Measure | Starting target | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Response time | First reply to new enquiries, requests and Zara handovers in working hours | ≤ 1 hour, 90% of the time | Messages, Requests |
| Follow-up discipline | Open leads with a next step and a date | ≥ 95% | Financial CRM |
| Qualified bookings | Qualified leads that book a first consultation or assessment | Agreed per quarter (to confirm) | Financial CRM |
| Handover quality | Clinical team rates handover notes complete (reason, history shared, preferences, consent) | ≥ 90% | Handover check in quarterly review |
| CRM accuracy | Lead source and stage recorded correctly in a sample | ≥ 95% | Quarterly CRM audit |
| Compliance | Call/chat review: no clinical claims, no cure claims, no pressure, privacy kept | 100% pass; any fail is reviewed | Quarterly call/chat review |
| Patient experience | Enquiry-to-booking satisfaction score | ≥ 4.3 average | Patient survey |
| Complaints and refunds | Upheld complaints about sales conduct; refunds where the patient says they were mis-sold | 0 upheld | Complaints log |

**Never used as targets for sales staff:** number of lab tests sold, supplement add-ons, upgrades to Elite or longer programmes, average basket size, or anything that depends on what a clinician recommends. The sales team books the first conversation; what a patient then needs is a clinical decision. Commission follows the same rule (see `compensation-and-payroll.md`).

### Admin, operations and other staff

Use the same structure: 4–6 KPIs drawn from their actual work (for example: payroll paid on time, access removed on leaver's last day, monthly access review done, supplier invoices paid within terms), agreed at goal setting.

### Behaviours (all staff)

Rated 1–5 alongside KPIs: puts the patient's interest first; honest about results and limits; protects patient data; reliable (time, follow-through); works well with colleagues across clinical and sales.

## 5. Mid-year and annual appraisal

**Mid-year (July).** 45-minute conversation. Review progress against each goal and KPI, agree what to change, update goals if the work has changed. No rating. Manager writes a short note in the HR system within 5 working days.

**Annual (December – January).**

1. Staff member completes a self-review (ratings and short comments per goal, KPI and behaviour) — by 10 December.
2. Manager gathers evidence: KPI figures, case or call reviews, patient feedback, peer input (2 colleagues, optional) — by 20 December.
3. Appraisal meeting (60 min) — by 15 January. Manager proposes ratings; both agree a development plan and next year's goals.
4. Calibration (February), then final rating shared in writing.
5. Staff member signs to confirm they have seen it (not that they agree) and can add comments.

**Disagreeing with a rating.** Within 10 working days of the final rating, the staff member can ask in writing for a review. The Admin (or another manager not involved) reviews the evidence, meets the staff member and decides within 10 working days. That decision is final for the appraisal; it does not affect any separate grievance right.

## 6. Performance Improvement Plans (PIP)

A PIP is for **capability** (the person is trying but not meeting the role). Misconduct (dishonesty, privacy breaches, unsafe practice, abuse of patients or colleagues) goes through the disciplinary process instead. Clinical safety concerns may need immediate supervision or restriction of practice while a PIP runs, and reporting to the relevant council where the law or the council's rules require it (confirm with counsel).

**Before a PIP**: the manager raises the concern in a 1:1, gives examples, and agrees informal support for at least 4 weeks. A PIP follows only if that does not work, or if the annual rating is 1.

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| Written plan | 2–4 specific gaps, what "good" looks like for each, how it will be measured, support offered (training, shadowing, reduced caseload) |
| Length | 60 days by default (30–90 depending on the gap) |
| Check-ins | Weekly, 30 minutes, written note each time, shared with the employee |
| Midpoint review | Day 30: on track / not on track, said plainly |
| Outcome | Passed (back to normal reviews, monitored for 3 months) / extended once by up to 30 days / not passed |
| Not passed | Move to the formal capability process: a hearing where the employee can respond and bring a colleague, then a decision (redeployment, final written warning, or termination per `offboarding.md`) (confirm with counsel) |

The employee receives a copy of the plan, every check-in note and the outcome. Nigerian courts, particularly the National Industrial Court, look at whether the employee was told about the problem and given a fair chance to respond before termination; keep the paper trail (confirm with counsel).

## 7. Records and privacy

- Appraisals, goals, PIPs and probation outcomes are personal data under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023. Visible only to the employee, their manager and the Admin/HR owner.
- Patient details in case reviews are referenced by record ID, not copied into appraisal forms.
- Keep appraisal and PIP records for the duration of employment plus 6 years (confirm with counsel).
