# FXMed Disciplinary and Grievance Procedure

This procedure explains how FXMed deals fairly with concerns about an employee's conduct or performance (disciplinary), and how employees can raise concerns about their work, colleagues or treatment (grievance). Its aim is to correct problems early, protect patients, and make sure every decision is based on facts, heard fairly and recorded. It applies to all employees after they join, including during probation (where a shortened process may be used, see 2.6). It does not form part of your contract, and FXMed may adjust timings where it is fair to do so. Termination and dismissal must also comply with the Labour Act, your contract and decisions of the National Industrial Court of Nigeria (confirm with counsel before any dismissal).

## 1. Principles of a fair procedure

1. **Informal first.** Minor issues are dealt with by a conversation with your supervisor, noted but not a formal warning.
2. **Investigate before deciding.** No formal action is taken until the facts have been looked at.
3. **Know the case.** You are told in writing what the concern is, and given copies of the evidence, before any hearing.
4. **Be heard.** You can respond, give your side, bring documents and suggest witnesses.
5. **Be accompanied.** You may bring a colleague, or a trade union representative if you are a member, to any formal hearing. They can speak for you but cannot answer questions for you. Legal representation is not usually allowed at internal hearings.
6. **Different people.** The investigator, the person deciding and the person hearing an appeal are different people wherever the size of the team allows. If the Managing Director has been involved earlier, an external HR adviser may hear the appeal.
7. **Consistency and proportion.** Similar cases get similar outcomes; the sanction fits the conduct, your record and any mitigation.
8. **Right of appeal** against any formal outcome.
9. **Confidentiality.** Records are kept on your HR file and seen only by those who need them. Patients involved in an investigation are protected under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023; their records are used only to the extent needed.
10. **No dismissal without a hearing,** except where summary dismissal is lawful and justified, and even then you will be given the chance to respond (confirm with counsel).

Reasonable adjustments are made for disability, health or language needs. Meetings can be rescheduled once if you or your companion cannot attend, usually within 5 working days.

## 2. Disciplinary procedure

### 2.1 Conduct or capability?

| Type | Example | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Misconduct** | Repeated lateness, rudeness, not following home-visit check-in rules | Disciplinary stages below |
| **Capability (performance)** | Notes regularly incomplete despite training, missed sales targets with no misconduct | Support first: clear standards, extra training (Training tab), supervision and a written improvement plan of 4–8 weeks; formal stages only if no improvement |
| **Capability (health)** | Ongoing illness affecting work | Supportive process under the Leave Policy; medical advice; adjustments. Not a disciplinary matter |
| **Gross misconduct** | See section 3 | Investigation, possible suspension, hearing, may lead to dismissal without notice |

### 2.2 Investigation

- HR appoints an investigator (usually a supervisor not involved in the matter) within 3 working days of a concern being raised.
- The investigator interviews the employee, witnesses and, where appropriate, the patient (with their consent), and gathers records: portal and EMR audit logs, calendar and check-in records, payment and dispensary records, messages.
- The investigation is usually completed within **10 working days**. The investigator writes a short report: the allegation, evidence, findings of fact, and a recommendation (no case to answer, informal action, or formal hearing).

### 2.3 Precautionary suspension

Where there is a risk to patients, evidence or other staff (for example alleged patient harm, theft of drugs, practising without a licence, data misuse), FXMed may suspend the employee **on full pay** while it investigates, or move them to non-patient-facing duties. Suspension is not a punishment and is not a finding of guilt. It is confirmed in writing, kept as short as possible and reviewed every 10 working days. Portal access may be suspended at the same time.

### 2.4 Hearing

- You get at least **3 working days'** written notice of the hearing, with the allegations, the evidence, the possible outcomes (including dismissal if that is possible), and your right to be accompanied.
- At the hearing the investigator presents the findings, you respond, and the chair can ask questions and adjourn to check anything new.
- The chair decides after the hearing, and confirms the outcome and reasons **in writing within 5 working days**, with how to appeal.

### 2.5 Stages and sanctions

| Stage | When used | Who decides | Live on file for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Informal note | Minor first issue | Supervisor | Noted only; not a sanction |
| **Stage 1: Written warning** | Misconduct, or failure to meet a performance improvement plan | Supervisor (with HR) | 6 months |
| **Stage 2: Final written warning** | Further misconduct while a warning is live, or a first offence serious enough to warrant it | Managing Director or Medical Director (with HR) | 12 months |
| **Stage 3: Dismissal with notice (or pay in lieu)** | Further misconduct while a final warning is live, or failure to improve | Managing Director | Permanent record |
| **Summary dismissal (without notice)** | Gross misconduct proven after a fair process | Managing Director | Permanent record |

Alternatives to dismissal at Stage 3 can include demotion, transfer to another role, or loss of a role-related privilege (for example, removal from unsupervised home visits), where agreed and lawful (confirm with counsel). FXMed does not use fines or salary deductions as a disciplinary sanction (confirm with counsel).

When a warning expires it is disregarded for future disciplinary decisions but stays on file.

### 2.6 Probation

During probation, concerns are raised at check-ins and recorded. A shortened process applies: a meeting with written notice of the concerns and a chance to respond, then a decision to extend probation or end employment with contractual notice. The right of appeal still applies.

### 2.7 Regulators and external reporting

Where conduct may amount to professional misconduct or put patients at risk, FXMed may have to report it to the relevant regulator (MDCN, NMCN, PCN, MLSCN or the Dietitians Council). Suspected crimes such as theft or assault may be reported to the police, and personal data breaches to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission. Internal proceedings can continue alongside these (confirm with counsel). The employee is told when a report is made unless doing so would prejudice an investigation.

## 3. Gross misconduct

Gross misconduct is conduct so serious that it destroys trust and may justify dismissal without notice for a first offence. It always goes through investigation and a hearing. Examples relevant to FXMed (not a complete list):

**Patient safety and clinical practice**

- Practising without a current licence or practising licence, or working outside the limits of your licence or role (for example, a non-clinician diagnosing or advising treatment).
- Deliberate or reckless conduct that harms or seriously endangers a patient.
- Prescribing, dispensing or administering medicines or supplements without authority, or diverting controlled drugs.
- Falsifying, deleting or back-dating clinical records, lab results, consent forms or visit check-ins.
- Sexual misconduct or any sexual or romantic relationship with a patient; examining a patient intimately without consent.
- Abuse or neglect of a vulnerable patient, including children in the Autism Support programme and elderly patients.
- Being under the influence of alcohol or drugs at work or on a home visit.
- Ignoring a safeguarding concern or failing to escalate a medical emergency.

**Data and confidentiality**

- Accessing patient records without a work reason, sharing patient data on personal WhatsApp or social media, or disclosing patient information without authority.
- Concealing a data breach.
- Sharing your login or using someone else's.

**Honesty and money**

- Theft or fraud, including of supplements, dispensary stock, lab kits, patient payments or expense claims.
- Taking patient payments into a personal account, or charging patients for "private" visits outside FXMed.
- Accepting bribes, kickbacks or commissions from labs, pharmacies, suppliers or HMOs.
- Giving false information in your job application (qualifications, licence, references).

**Behaviour**

- Violence, threats or serious bullying of patients, families or colleagues.
- Sexual harassment or discrimination.
- Victimising someone who raised a grievance or a whistleblowing concern.
- Serious breach of health and safety rules, including the lone-working rules, that puts people at risk.
- Serious insubordination: refusing a reasonable and lawful instruction.
- Bringing FXMed into serious disrepute, for example by publishing patient stories or making cure claims on social media.

## 4. Appeals (disciplinary)

1. Appeal **in writing to HR within 5 working days** of receiving the outcome, saying why (for example: procedure not followed, new evidence, sanction too severe).
2. The appeal is heard by someone more senior who was not involved, or by an external HR adviser if no such person exists, normally within **10 working days**.
3. You may be accompanied, as at the hearing.
4. The appeal can uphold, reduce or overturn the decision. It cannot increase the sanction.
5. The appeal outcome is confirmed in writing within 5 working days and is final within FXMed. You may still have rights to take the matter to the National Industrial Court of Nigeria or through mediation (confirm with counsel).

If you are dismissed, the dismissal takes effect on the original date while the appeal is heard; if the appeal succeeds, you are reinstated with back pay.

## 5. Grievance procedure

A grievance is a concern, problem or complaint about your work, working conditions or relationships at work: for example workload, rotas, leave decisions, pay errors, bullying, harassment, discrimination or safety. Concerns about wrongdoing in the public interest (patient safety, fraud) go through Whistleblowing in the [Employee Handbook](employee-handbook.md). Concerns about a decision in a disciplinary case go through the disciplinary appeal.

### 5.1 Stages

| Stage | What happens | Who | Time limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Informal** | Talk to your supervisor (or HR if the issue is about your supervisor). Most issues are sorted here. | Supervisor or HR | Response within 5 working days |
| **Formal: Stage 1** | Write to HR setting out the grievance and what would resolve it. A meeting is held; you may be accompanied. The manager investigates if needed. | HR plus a manager not involved | Meeting within 5 working days of the written grievance; written outcome within 5 working days of the meeting |
| **Formal: Stage 2 (appeal)** | If not satisfied, appeal in writing within 5 working days. | Managing Director, or an external HR adviser if the Managing Director is involved | Meeting within 10 working days; written outcome within 5 working days; final within FXMed |

- **Harassment and discrimination complaints** go straight to the formal stage if you prefer, and may be raised with HR or the Managing Director directly.
- **Mediation** may be offered for disputes between colleagues, if both agree.
- **Collective grievances** (several staff, same issue) may be raised together and heard together.
- If a grievance is raised during a disciplinary process, the disciplinary may be paused briefly, or both dealt with together, whichever is fairer.
- You will not be treated worse for raising a grievance in good faith.

## 6. Records

| Record | Kept by | Retention (confirm with counsel) |
|---|---|---|
| Informal notes | Supervisor, in the HR system | Until 12 months after the note |
| Investigation reports, hearing notes, outcome letters | HR | Length of employment + 6 years |
| Warnings | HR | Live periods as in 2.5; kept on file for the length of employment + 6 years |
| Grievance records and outcomes | HR | Length of employment + 6 years |
| Regulator or police referrals | HR and Medical Director | Length of employment + 6 years |

Employees can request a copy of the records held about them under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023. Notes are shared with the employee after each formal meeting.
